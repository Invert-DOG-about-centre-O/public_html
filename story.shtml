Our Story

CUEiMS was founded in 2016 in the aftermath of a series of events where actions by mathematicians and the (mis)use of mathematical work caused widespread societal harm. Many students and academics came to be of the view that a contributing factor to these events was a lack of formal ethical training for mathematicians. In an age of information where mathematicians and mathematical tools had become much sought after, those with the power to create useful mathematics also needed the expertise to produce and wield these responsibly. In particular, there was need for discourse on the specific ethical issues that pertain to the unique technical aspects of a practising mathematician's work. CUEiMS was formed to fill this gap.

Dr Maurice Chiodo, who co-founded the Ethics in Mathematics Project, delivered the first ever Ethics in Mathematics seminars in 2016. Since then, CUEiMS has hosted these seminars every Michaelmas in the form of an 8-lecture course, ordinarily in the Centre for Mathematical Sciences, but also online during the COVID years of 2020 and 2021. These lectures have largely held a format of: first prove there exists 'Ethics' in 'Mathematics', then argue for all 'Mathematics', there exists 'Ethics'.

Over the years, CUEiMS has also hosted talks and discussions with an impressive range of highly distinguished speakers, including no less than Edward Snowden, Julian Assange and William Binney. Such events have played an important role in the Faculty, raising awareness about crucial ethical aspects of doing mathematics, and we continue to host many such talks every year.

In Lent 2024, we introduced the EiM-Advanced course, an advanced discussion course in which we discuss the ethics of a certain problem with mathematical aspects over 10 sessions. Topics for these have been 'AI-powered bus timetabling', 'building an oil pipe', and 'building an encrypted messaging app'. The purpose of this course is to teach mathematicians who already have an understanding of topics from the Michaelmas course a toolkit on how to carry out mathematical work ethically and responsibly.

Despite our rather serious side, CUEiMS has always been a vibrant and social community. Traditions include pub visits at the end of Michaelmas and Lent to celebrate the term, society visits to the Cambridge Beer Festival every Easter, and waiting a week (more like a day these days) when in search for a real-world EiM example. We make sure to fill our calendars with fun socials, but even our seminars tend to become social events in themselves due to the hour for discussion at the end.
